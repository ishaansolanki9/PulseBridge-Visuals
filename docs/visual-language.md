# Visual language

The performance display is treated as a room-scale light source, not a software interface. It always renders edge-to-edge color and motion with no labels, meters, transport controls, logos, or diagnostic overlays.

## Styles

- **Fluid** uses layered noise fields and slow color advection. Bass changes the scale and mass of the forms; mids create lateral movement; highs add restrained detail.
- **Waves** combines several luminous sinusoidal ribbons. Beat energy changes their displacement and glow while the lines remain continuous rather than becoming a chart.
- **Pulse** uses expanding radial rings and geometric energy fields. Onsets create quick spatial releases without requiring a white frame.
- **Tunnel** creates perspective tension and accelerating depth during builds.
- **Burst** creates radial impact fronts and colored release energy for major drops.
- **Auto** crossfades between all five styles according to musical state instead of making abrupt preset cuts.

## Color

Every palette contains four related colors: a dark foundation, two primary light colors, and an accent. Automatic color direction favors Ocean during Quiet, Flow, and Breakdown; Electric during Groove; Sunset during Build; and Neon during Impact and Peak. Fixed choices also include Purple + blue, Warm, Monochrome, and Rainbow flow.

## Motion and safety

Chill, Balanced, and Wild scale brightness, detail, motion, and impact together. Attack envelopes allow beats and impacts to arrive quickly; slower releases keep the image from flickering. White flash is opt-in at Off, Moderate, or High. Off keeps impacts colorful and spatial while suppressing the white-frame component.

The vignette is intentional: it prevents a television from reading as a flat white rectangle in a dark room while preserving strong center and edge-to-edge movement.
